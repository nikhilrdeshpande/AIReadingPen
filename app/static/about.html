AI Reading Pen

A pointing device for children who know some Devanagari letters but cannot yet blend them into words.

The bigger idea

Millions of children in India learn to read Marathi and Hindi with very little one-to-one help. The missing piece is not content, it is a patient adult who, for any word the child points at, says the word, breaks it into the sounds the child already knows, blends them back, and then listens to the child try. A reading pen puts that adult inside a small camera the child can hold over any book, worksheet, or signboard.

What this demo proves

Point and hearA XIAO ESP32S3 camera reads one printed word and the lesson starts in under 200 ms.
Teach, don't just readLessons follow the barakhadi method teachers use: झ ला काना, झा. Reviewed for 24 words, generated for any other real word.
Listen backThe child says the word; speech recognition checks it and the pen praises or asks again.
Never inventFragments and misreads are refused. A wrong word is worse than "try again" in a literacy product.

What the product would add

  • On-device recognition and speech, so the pen works without a laptop or network.
  • A speaker in the pen and a small screen or light ring for "hold steady" feedback.
  • Pointing at one word inside a full page, not a single-word card.
  • Teacher-reviewed lesson packs per grade, with progress a parent or teacher can see.
  • More scripts: the same pipeline applies to Gujarati, Kannada, Tamil and others.
