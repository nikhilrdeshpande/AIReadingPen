AI Reading Pen · research note · 23 September 2026

What should the pen run on?

What commercial reading and dictionary pens are built on, which chips can run Devanagari OCR on-device today, and a recommendation for the next prototype. Constraint: no screen for the child, no phone.

Short answer. Every offline dictionary pen on the market is a small Linux computer with a 0.4 to 1 TOPS NPU, not a microcontroller. The chip Youdao ships in its current flagship, Rockchip RK3562, and its cheaper cousin RV1106 run the same PaddleOCR family we used today through Rockchip's RKNN toolchain. The next prototype should be a Luckfox Pico Ultra W (RV1106G3, about $30) to prove the Devanagari recognizer on a 1 TOPS NPU, with RK3562 as the production-class target. ESP32-class parts are not enough yet.

Two takeaways. First, the shipping pens with real OCR all sit at roughly the same point: a Cortex-A class Linux SoC with a small NPU, 128 MB to 1 GB of RAM, a camera, a speaker, dual mics, and a 1200 mAh cell. Second, every one of them has a screen, because they sell to adults and older students. A screen-free pen for six-year-olds is a genuinely open position.

What matters: an NPU that can run PaddleOCR's mobile recognizer (the model we used today, about 10 MB, 50 ms on a laptop), a camera interface, enough RAM for Linux, and a toolchain that converts Paddle models without a research project.

What we don't know yet, and how to find out in one session

  • Real latency on a 1 TOPS NPU. Nobody publishes PP-OCR recognizer timings for RV1106 or RK3562. On the laptop the model took 50 ms; int8 on the NPU should land between 30 and 150 ms, CPU-only on a Cortex-A7 nearer one second. Measure it, don't assume.
  • Devanagari model conversion. The RKNN examples use the Chinese/English recognizer. Our model, devanagari_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec, must go Paddle → ONNX → RKNN with int8 quantization. The saved frames from today are the calibration and test set.
  • Accuracy after quantization on the 24 cards, using the same gate rules. Today's 4-of-4 demo cards and the near-miss rejection are the bar.

The rest of the pen

Speech outNo usable offline Marathi or Hindi TTS exists at this size. Ship pre-generated audio for the grade vocabulary (a few thousand words, a few hundred MB) and fetch only new words. Speaker + class-D amp: about $2.
Speech inWhisper-tiny or a Vosk Hindi model on a Cortex-A53 can verify a single word in about a second. On an A7 it will be slow; keep the cloud check as the fallback there.
Feedback without a screenA light ring for "hold steady / captured" and a two-tone chime. All of today's UI states map to light and sound.
OpticsFixed-focus camera at a known distance, a small skirt on the nose to fix the distance and shade the page. Repeatable geometry beat every model tweak today.
Power1000–1250 mAh cell, as in the shipping pens. RV1106 idles low; RK3562 needs more care.
Cloud footprintOnly new-word lessons and validation, asynchronous and cached forever. A few hundred small calls per pen per month: a small plan covers a classroom.

Recommended next session

  • Order a Luckfox Pico Ultra W (RV1106G3) with its camera, and, if budget allows, an RK3562 core board.
  • Convert the Devanagari recognizer to RKNN int8 and benchmark it on today's saved frames. Success is under 200 ms per word with the four demo cards passing.
  • Port the capture controller and gate as they are; they are pure Python and OpenCV.
  • Only then decide RV1106 versus RK3562 on measured latency and power.
